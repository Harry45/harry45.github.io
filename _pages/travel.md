## Africa

### South Africa

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Africa/SKASA2015.jpg" caption="SKA SA Conference, 2015" width=400 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">I spent around eighteen months in Cape Town, during which I attended a range of conferences and workshops and met many inspiring people. The experience also introduced me to a rich variety of cultures and traditions.</p>

<p align="justify">The SKA SA (Square Kilometre Array South Africa) conference is held annually. Students funded through SKA SA bursaries attend to present their research, through both talks and poster sessions.</p>

<p align="justify">The <a href="https://www.skatelescope.org/">Square Kilometre Array</a> (SKA) will be the world's largest radio telescope and will be located partly on the African continent. A key aim of bringing together students from across Africa is to develop human capital throughout the continent.</p>



{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Africa/BodhiKhaya.jpg" caption="Bodhi Khaya, South Africa" width=418 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">Another memorable experience was a week spent at <a href="http://www.bodhi-khaya.co.za/">Bodhi Khaya</a>. One evening, we gathered around the fire and shared the paths that had brought us there. Each story was different, but a common thread stood out: motivation and determination had carried everyone through difficult circumstances.</p>

<p align="justify">We organised a JEDI workshop on machine learning at Bodhi Khaya, while participants were also free to pursue their own research. One idea we explored was generating music with a deep neural network. Although we made limited progress, around a year later DeepMind released <a href="https://deepmind.com/blog/wavenet-generative-model-raw-audio/">WaveNet</a>, which uses deep learning to generate audio, including music. <a href="http://astro.ic.ac.uk/ajaffe/home">Prof. Andrew Jaffe</a> also visited from Imperial College London, and together we started a new BIRO (Bayesian Inference for Radio Observations) project, which aimed to use visibilities to infer the separation between two point sources buried in noise.</p>

### Mozambique

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Africa/Mozambique.JPG" caption="JEDI workshop, Mozambique, 2016" width=400 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">In September 2016, we organised a week-long JEDI workshop in Mozambique, the first event of its kind held in the country. I was initially hesitant, given the health precautions required (malaria prophylaxis and yellow fever vaccination), but the opportunity to contribute to this first event persuaded me to take part. Communication was a challenge, as nine of the ten participants were Portuguese speakers, yet everyone made a real effort to work in English. In one networking session, each participant spoke with three people they had not met before in about fifteen minutes. During that activity, I met a young man who explained that Steve Jobs was his role model and a constant source of motivation; his story left a lasting impression on me. Over the course of the week, participants learned about cosmology by attempting to reproduce the results behind the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics.</p>

### Mauritius

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Africa/MLJedi_2015.jpg" caption="Machine Learning JEDI, Mauritius, 2015" width=450 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">The <a href="https://sites.google.com/a/ska.ac.za/ml-jedi-mauritius/home">Mauritius Machine Learning JEDI</a> took place from 16 to 26 July 2015 at <a href="http://www.villasun.mu/">Villasun</a>, with generous funding from SKA SA and the Newton Fund. We worked alongside experts including <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/jasper-horrell-64a93937">Dr Jasper Horrell</a> (Science Processing Manager at SKA SA) and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/datamusing">Dr Sudeep Das</a> (Senior Researcher at <a href="https://www.netflix.com/">Netflix</a>, formerly a data scientist at <a href="http://www.opentable.com/start/home">OpenTable</a>), among other researchers.</p>

<p align="justify">The participants, mostly undergraduates from the science and engineering faculties of the University of Mauritius, tackled two imaging problems. The first, from radio astronomy, involved counting the point sources and extended sources in a sky image, for which a friends-of-friends (FoF) algorithm was proposed. The second was diabetic retinopathy detection, based on a <a href="https://www.kaggle.com/c/diabetic-retinopathy-detection">Kaggle</a> competition.</p>

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Africa/Jedi2016_Mauritius.jpg" caption="Astro Data Science miniJEDI, 2016" width=400 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">Many Mauritian students are studying in South Africa thanks to financial support from SKA SA and other funding agencies. Each year, on returning to Mauritius, we organise a short workshop to share our knowledge with students there. For example, I served on the organising committee of the <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/minijedi2016/home">Astro Data Science miniJEDI</a>, held from 4 to 8 January 2016.</p>

<p align="justify">The first few days introduced participants to Python programming. I gave a session on presentation skills, inspired by the book <i>The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs</i>, as well as an introductory talk on Bayesian statistics. In the final two days, participants worked on data analysis and exoplanet detection, gaining hands-on coding experience.</p>

## Europe

### Germany

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/Lindau_Press_Good.jpg" caption="AI press talk, Lindau, 2016" width=520 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">I was one of 400 young scientists selected to attend the 66<sup>th</sup> <a href="http://www.lindau-nobel.org/">Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting</a> in Germany, which brought together 31 Nobel laureates for informal discussions with participants. I also took part in several side events, including the Africa Outreach Breakfast and a session on Excellence in Science and Sports. In addition, I visited <a href="http://www.conti-online.com/generator/www/start/com/en/index_en.html">Continental</a>, where I learned how machine learning techniques were being tested for car braking systems, and attended a discussion on the challenges and opportunities of big data.</p>

<p align="justify">I also took part in a press talk with Vint Cerf, Google's Vice President and Chief Internet Evangelist. Organised by the <a href="http://www.faz.net/">Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung</a> (FAZ), the discussion focused on how artificial intelligence may shape our lives.</p>

### Switzerland
{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/Geneva.jpg" caption="Geneva, Switzerland" width=400 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">In 2015 and 2016, I visited my co-supervisor, <a href="http://cosmology.unige.ch/users/martin-kunz">Prof. Martin Kunz</a>, at the University of Geneva, where I was able to communicate comfortably in French, one of the languages spoken in Mauritius.</p>

<p align="justify">The purpose of these visits was to advance our joint research. In 2015, at the start of the project, we were still working out why the MCMC analysis was failing in one particular case; by 2016, we were writing the paper together.</p>

<p align="justify">At the time, I was completing my Master's degree, and I met several PhD students at the university who shared valuable advice about undertaking a PhD. These included David Daverio, one of Martin's PhD students at the time.</p>

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/IDIAP.jpg" caption="Idiap Research Institute, Martigny" width=450 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">In 2015, I visited the <a href="https://www.idiap.ch/">Idiap</a> Research Institute, where I met James Newling, a former Master's student of Prof. Bruce Bassett. James was completing his PhD in machine learning with <a href="https://www.idiap.ch/~fleuret/">François Fleuret</a> at Idiap and <a href="https://www.epfl.ch/">EPFL</a> (École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne).</p>

<p align="justify">James invited me to a talk he gave on his research in François' office, attended by members of the group. It was a privilege to meet François afterwards. In a conversation following the talk, James's key message was simple: "research is hard!"</p>

<p align="justify">At the time, I was keen to pursue a PhD in machine learning, and Idiap appealed to me greatly. It is set in a peaceful location surrounded by mountains, and my French would have made it easy to settle in.</p>


{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/ETH.JPG" caption="ETH Zurich, Switzerland" width=400 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">During my 2016 visit to Geneva, I also took the opportunity to visit the <a href="http://www.ml.inf.ethz.ch/">Institute for Machine Learning</a> at ETH Zurich. As my visit fell during the holiday period, I was unable to meet faculty members, but I did speak with several PhD students.</p>

<p align="justify">I met <a href="http://olivierbachem.ch/">Olivier Bachem</a>, <a href="https://ch.linkedin.com/in/baharan-mirzasoleiman-0858b991">Baharan Mirzasoleiman</a>, <a href="https://ch.linkedin.com/in/nico-gorbach-69045b38">Nico Gorbach</a> and <a href="http://people.inf.ethz.ch/ybian/">Yatao Bian</a>. Nico had completed his undergraduate and Master's studies at the University of Cape Town, so he understood my background well. Together, they gave me a clear picture of what a machine learning PhD at ETH Zurich involves, including the expectation that PhD students also contribute to teaching.</p>

<p align="justify">I was particularly interested in deep learning, which was a focus of the <a href="http://www.da.inf.ethz.ch/">Data Analytics Lab</a>, although I did not have the opportunity to visit the group.</p>

### United Kingdom

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/London.jpg" caption="Emirates Stadium, London" width=400 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">I also visited Dr Michelle Lochner, then a postdoctoral researcher in the <a href="http://www.ucl.ac.uk/star">UCL Astrophysics Group</a>. Michelle completed her PhD with Prof. Bruce Bassett and was among the first students to work on the BIRO project, which originated at a JEDI workshop. We discussed statistical methods, including those she applied in the <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0611004">BEAMS</a> paper.</p>

<p align="justify">In 2016, before attending the summer school in Chania, I visited <a href="http://astro.ic.ac.uk/ajaffe/home">Prof. Andrew Jaffe</a> at Imperial College London. We continued our work on Bayesian methods in radio astronomy, and I met several PhD students who encouraged me to apply to the department.</p>

### Greece

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/Cosmo21_group.jpg" caption="COSMO21 workshop, Chania, 2016" width=390 align="right" %}

<p align="justify">The <a href="http://cosmo21.cosmostat.org/">COSMO21</a> workshop, Statistical Challenges in 21st Century Cosmology, was held in Chania immediately after the <a href="http://ada8.cosmostat.org/">ADA8</a> Astronomical Data Analysis Summer School. I found the format of a summer school followed by a conference particularly valuable, as it deepened my understanding of Bayesian methods and sparsity. It was also my first opportunity to meet <a href="http://robertotrotta.com/">Dr Roberto Trotta</a>, from whose lectures and well-crafted review, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4089">Bayes in the sky</a>, I had first learned Bayesian statistics.</p>

<p align="justify">The conference featured many excellent talks and a poster session, and the prize for best <a href="http://cosmo21.cosmostat.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/poster_cs21_KUNTZER_light.pdf">poster</a> went to a machine learning project. In the closing session, it was agreed that the next summer school would include a dedicated tutorial on machine learning.</p>

### France

{% include image.html url="/images/travel/Europe/LesHouches_All_People.jpg" caption="Astrostatistics school, Les Houches, 2015" width=400 align="left" %}

<p align="justify">I also attended the <a href="https://stat4astro2015.sciencesconf.org/">School of Astrostatistics</a> at the École de Physique des Houches. The school focused on clustering and classification, with the aim of bridging the gap between astronomers and statisticians, a long-standing topic of debate in the astronomy community. The programme included talks and tutorials on Bayesian statistics and machine learning in R.</p>

<p align="justify">This was also my first experience of snow. We visited the summit of the Aiguille du Midi, which, coming from Mauritius, where the average temperature is around 25&nbsp;&deg;C, felt extremely cold.</p>
